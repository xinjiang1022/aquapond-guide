How to Measure Your Koi Pond Volume

Water Quality~8 min readUpdated Nov 4, 2026

"How big is my pond?" is a question you only ask when you need to add salt, dose a treatment, or buy a pump — and by then guessing can over-dose fish or undersize equipment. Measuring volume takes a few minutes, and it is the reference number behind almost every other decision in pond keeping.

Why your pond volume matters

Every dosing guide, salt rate and pump recommendation is written per litre or per gallon of water. Without a volume, you are working blind. It feeds into:

  • Salt dosing — see Koi Pond Salt: When and How Much
  • Medication and parasite treatments (fish health)
  • Pump and filter sizing — see Choosing a Pond Pump
  • Water-change planning — see Water Change Guide

The basic method

Measure the water surface, not the hole — measure to the water line. Depth is the average depth, measured in a few spots. Then use the formula for your shape:

Pond shapeFormula
Rectangular / squareLength × width × average depth
Round / oval(π × radius²) × average depth ≈ 0.79 × diameter² × depth
Irregular natural pondSplit into two or three rectangles, measure each, add them up

That gives you cubic metres or cubic feet. Convert: 1 cubic metre = 1000 litres; 1 cubic foot ≈ 7.5 US gallons. Always multiply by a small safety factor for the shelves and ledges you forgot — treat the estimate as a working number, not a precision measurement.

TipMost ponds are shallower at the edges. Measure depth at the shallow shelf and at the deep end, then use the average — otherwise you will overestimate the volume and under-dose.

Worked example

Say your pond is roughly rectangular: 3 m long, 2 m wide, average depth 1.2 m. Volume = 3 × 2 × 1.2 = 7.2 cubic metres = about 7,200 litres. If it is oval instead, measure the widest point across (diameter), square it, multiply by the average depth and by 0.79. If it is a free-form natural pond, walk around it with a tape and sketch three overlapping rectangles on paper — measure each, calculate each, then add the three results.

Once you have the litre figure, convert to gallons when following a product label: multiply litres by about 0.22 to get UK gallons, or by about 0.26 to get US gallons — check which gallon the label uses before you trust the conversion.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring the liner hole, not the water.If the pond runs half-full, measure half.
  • Forgetting the waterfall and filter chamber.If your filter holds a lot of water, add a rough estimate to the total volume when dosing treatments.
  • Rounding up "about a tonne".A 20% error in volume is a 20% error in every dose afterwards.

Write the number on the filter shed wall. You will use it again this season — for salt, for a treatment, and when you wonder why your pump seems to struggle. Cross-check your chemistry afterwards with the weekly testing routine.
