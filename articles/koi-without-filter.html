Can Koi Live Without a Filter?

Pond Setup~7 min readUpdated Nov 17, 2026

"Just plants and nature will take care of it" — it sounds appealing, but the reality is more nuanced. Koi can live in ponds that look natural, yet those ponds are rarely truly unfiltered. Understanding what a filter does helps you decide whether you actually need one. (See the basics in pond filtration.)

What a filter actually does

A pond filter is not a magic box that cleans water on its own. Its real job is to house the beneficial bacteria that process fish waste into less harmful forms (see the nitrogen cycle), and to give solids somewhere to settle or be removed. If those bacteria are present elsewhere — in gravel, on plant roots, on the liner itself — some of that work still gets done.

Can koi survive with no filter

SetupReality
Small pond, many koi, no filterAlmost always ends in poor water quality and stressed fish
Large pond, very few koi, lots of plantsCan remain stable for a while — but fish load must stay low
"Natural" wildlife pondNot designed for koi; koi produce far more waste than most native fish
Key pointWith no filter, the limiting factor is how many koi you keep, not how the pond looks. A pretty pond with too many fish and no filter will crash.

Why beginners usually need one

For most people setting up their first koi pond, a properly sized filter removes the guesswork. Koi grow large and produce a lot of waste (see stocking density). Skipping filtration to save money often costs more later in lost fish and emergency clean-ups. Think of the filter as insurance for a living, growing animal.
