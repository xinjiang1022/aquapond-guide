How to Feed Koi in Cold Water: Fall and Winter Feeding Guide

Koi Care~9 min readUpdated Oct 12, 2026

Koi are cold-blooded: as the water cools, their digestion slows to a crawl. Feeding a warm-water diet in cold water is one of the most common ways ponds end up sick in spring. This guide explains exactly when to switch food, when to stop, and how to restart safely.

Why feeding must change with temperature

Koi digestion depends on water temperature — their metabolism follows the water, not the calendar. In warm water they process food quickly; as the temperature falls, digestion slows, and undigested food rots in the gut or pollutes the pond. That’s why the feeding plan should track a thermometer, not the date.

The golden ruleFeed the fish, not the calendar. A warm October week and a cold September one deserve different feeding — measure the water, not the month.

The temperature feeding stages

Temperatures below are general guidance — exact values vary with fish size, pond depth and local climate, so treat them as a starting framework and watch your fish:

Water tempWhat to feedHow often
Above 15°C (59°F)Normal high-protein growth foodNormal routine (see feeding guide)
~13–15°C (55–59°F)Wheatgerm or low-protein cold-water foodOnce a day, small amounts
~8–12°C (46–54°F)Wheatgerm only, very sparinglyEvery 2–3 days, tiny portions
Below 8°C (46°F)Stop feeding entirely—

Wheatgerm foods are easier to digest at low temperatures because they’re lower in protein and fat. Switch gradually over a week or two, mixing the new food in with the old, rather than changing overnight.

Fall feeding: the transition window

  • Start switching at ~15°C (55°F) on the way down — mix wheatgerm in gradually.
  • Watch for cold snaps: if the water plunges quickly, skip feeding on cold days rather than forcing food the fish can’t digest.
  • Feed at the warmest time of day — midday after the sun has lifted the water a degree or two.
  • Never feed when fish aren’t active: if koi are sluggish at the bottom, they’re telling you the food is not welcome yet.

Fall is also the time to ease off entirely as temperatures trend down — a gentle, staged reduction beats a sudden stop when winter arrives (winter care guide).

Winter: stop, don’t snack

Below roughly 8°C (46°F), koi’s metabolism slows so far that food cannot be digested properly:

  • Stop feeding completely — koi live off stored reserves through winter; this is normal and healthy.
  • Resist the temptation to “just give them a pellet” on warm winter days — a single undigested meal can cause problems days later.
  • Keep the filter running but reduce bio load expectations — bacteria also slow in the cold.
  • Maintain an air pump to keep an open hole in any ice — oxygen matters more than food in winter (aeration guide).

Spring: the risky restart

Spring is when feeding mistakes hit hardest — fish are weakest after winter and the filter is barely active. Restart carefully:

  1. Wait for stable temperatures above ~10°C (50°F) — not just one warm day, but a settled trend.
  2. Offer tiny amounts of wheatgerm first, every few days; watch whether it’s eaten and digested.
  3. Test ammonia and nitrite frequently — the filter wakes up slowly, and any food left over adds load (parameters guide).
  4. Only move back to growth food once water is consistently above ~15°C (59°F) and fish are feeding eagerly.
Key insightIn spring, an underfed koi is safer than an overfed one. Every pellet you withhold is water-quality insurance while the filter rebuilds.

A season-by-season feeding cheat sheet

SeasonFeedFrequency
Late fallWheatgerm mixOnce daily, decreasing
WinterNothing—

Late spring / summerNormal growth foodNormal routine

Cold-water feeding is really about one habit: watch the thermometer and match the fish. Get that right and your koi enter every season with a healthy gut, clean water and the reserves they need.
