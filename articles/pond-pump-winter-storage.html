Pond Pump Winter Storage: Remove or Leave It?

Gear & Equipment~7 min readUpdated Nov 9, 2026

Every autumn the same question comes up: should you pull the pump out for winter, or leave it running? The honest answer depends on how your pond overwinters — not on a universal rule. Here is how to decide, and what to do either way.

Do you actually need to remove the pump?

SituationWhat to do
Koi kept over winter in a deep pondLeave a pump running to keep an open hole in the ice — see winter depth
Fish removed / pond drained for winterRemove, clean and store the pump
Mild winters, pond never fully freezesUsually fine to leave running
Harsh winters with thick iceEither keep water moving near the surface, or store the pump
TipA pump sitting in frozen water can crack or seize. The decision is really: are the fish staying through winter, or not?

If you store it: how to do it right

Before temperatures drop, pull the pump, disconnect the hose, and flush it with clean water to remove muck. Let it drain fully — any water left inside is what freezes and cracks the housing. Store it somewhere frost-free (a garage or shed, not outdoors), and keep it submerged or covered in water if the manufacturer recommends it — this keeps the seals soft over winter. Check the impeller for leaves or debris before you pack it away.

If you leave it: what to watch

A running pump keeps a small area of surface from freezing, which helps gases escape — part of the normal winter setup (see winter care and pond heaters). Keep an eye that ice does not block the outflow, and clear snow off the pond so light still reaches the fish. If a hard freeze is forecast and you are not confident the water will keep moving, it is safer to store the pump than to risk it freezing in place.
