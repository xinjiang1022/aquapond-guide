Why Pond Water Turns Green After Rain

Water Quality~7 min readUpdated Nov 13, 2026

Your pond was clear yesterday, a storm rolled through overnight, and today the water is pea-soup green. This is one of the most predictable — and most often over-reacted-to — events in pond keeping. Rain does not "break" your pond; it usually just kicks off a short algae bloom that sorts itself out.

Why rain triggers it

What rain doesWhy it turns the water green
Washes nutrients in from the gardenAlgae has a fresh food source (see algae control)
Stirs bottom muck and runoffClouds the water and releases stored nutrients
Drops water temperature suddenlySlightly stresses fish and the established balance
TipGreen water after rain is usually a bloom of single-cell algae — not the same as the stringy algae that grows on walls (see the difference).

Is it dangerous

A short green bloom after rain is generally not dangerous to healthy koi — it looks alarming more than it harms. The time to take it more seriously is if the water stays green for weeks, the fish are gasping, or the bloom is paired with murky, smelly water (see murky water). Otherwise, patience plus normal maintenance is the right response.

How to clear it

The usual levers: keep filtration running, reduce feeding while the water is unsettled, and let the pond's own balance re-establish. Heavy, fast fixes are rarely needed the first time. A pond that repeatedly blooms after every rain usually has a nutrient-overload issue to address, not a one-off weather event.
