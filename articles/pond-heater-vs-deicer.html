Pond Heater vs De-Icer: Which Do You Need?

Winter Care~6 min readUpdated Nov 19, 2026

As winter approaches, pond owners shop for "pond heaters" without realizing the name covers two very different devices. One keeps a breathing hole in the ice; the other tries to warm the whole pond. Knowing which you actually need saves money and keeps the fish safer. (See koi pond heater and winter care.)

What a de-icer does

A de-icer (or pond heater in its common winter form) keeps a small area of the surface from freezing solid. Its real job is to leave an open breathing hole so gases can exchange between the water and the air — carbon dioxide and other wastes can escape, oxygen can enter. It does not keep the whole pond warm, and that is deliberate.

What a heater does

DeviceMain purposeTypical use
De-icer / ice preventerKeep one open spot in the iceMost koi ponds in cold climates
Full pond heaterHold the whole water volume at a warm temperatureSmall heated tanks, rare for outdoor koi
Key pointKoi are meant to slow down in cold water. Trying to keep an outdoor pond warm all winter is expensive, unnatural, and can interfere with their normal cold-season cycle. Depth matters more: a deep pond stays warmer than a shallow one (see depth in winter).

Which do you need

For most outdoor koi ponds in areas that freeze, the answer is a de-icer that keeps an opening in the ice, plus good depth and aeration. A full heater is usually reserved for small water features or special situations. If you are unsure, focus on keeping the gas exchange open rather than chasing a warm number on the thermometer.

Setup and safety

Whatever device you use, place it where it keeps an opening in the ice rather than heating the whole pond, and keep the cable and fittings above the waterline where you can inspect them. Use equipment rated for outdoor pond use, protected against frost and moisture, and follow the manufacturer's depth and placement guidance. If your power can fail in a cold spell, plan a backup rather than relying on the heater alone.
