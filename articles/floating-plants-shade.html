Best Floating Plants for Pond Shade

Pond Plants~6 min readUpdated Nov 16, 2026

A pond in full summer sun heats up and turns green fast. Floating plants are one of the easiest ways to put shade on the water without building anything: they drift, they grow, and they block the sunlight that fuels algae. (See the wider topic in pond shade.)

Why floating plants help

By covering part of the surface, they lower direct sun exposure, which helps keep water cooler and limits the light algae need (see algae control). They also take up some nutrients from the water. The aim is partial cover, not a solid mat across the whole pond.

Common options

PlantWhat it offers
Water lily (floating leaves)Classic shade, flowers, and cover fish like to rest under
Duckweed-type plantsFast cover, very small leaves — but can multiply quickly
Other floating nativesLight surface cover; check what is allowed in your area
NoteSome floating plants are considered invasive in certain regions. Check local rules before introducing any species, and never dump pond plants into natural waterways.

Keeping them under control

Floating plants grow — that is the point, but it can get away from you. Thin them out regularly so roughly half to two-thirds of the surface stays open for gas exchange and so the fish still have light. Koi may nibble some of them (see plants koi leave alone), which is part of the balance.

How much cover is ideal

Aim for roughly a third to half of the surface covered, not a complete carpet. Too little and you get little shade benefit; too much and the surface cannot exchange gas properly, and the fish lose the light they are used to. Because floating plants grow fastest in warm weather, check them weekly in summer and scoop the excess out rather than letting them mat over the whole pond.
