Pond Algae Control: How to Fix Green Water and String Algae

Water Quality~10 min readUpdated Sep 14, 2026

Algae is not the enemy — it is a symptom. When a pond turns pea-soup green or the walls grow fuzzy string algae, something is out of balance. Fix the imbalance and the algae takes care of itself. Here is how to diagnose and fix both common types.

First, know which algae you are fighting

Pond algae comes in two very different forms, and they need different fixes:

TypeWhat it looks likeWhat drives itBest fix
Green water (free-floating)Water turns cloudy green, like pea soup; fish can disappear from viewSingle-celled algae suspended in the water; driven by nutrients + sunlightUV clarifier, plant competition, less feeding, water changes
String algae (filamentous)Long green threads on walls, rocks, plants and the pond floorAttached algae; driven by excess nutrients and warm, sunny shallowsManual removal, shade, nutrient control, mechanical removal

Why algae blooms happen

Every algae bloom needs three ingredients:

  • Nutrients — nitrate and phosphate produced by fish waste and uneaten food. Overstocking and overfeeding are the classic drivers (see stocking density and feeding).
  • Light — algae photosynthesise; a pond in full summer sun with no shade is a greenhouse for algae.
  • No competition — a bare pond with no plants leaves all the nutrients for algae.
Think of it this wayAlgae is the pond’s way of telling you there is leftover food in the water. Kill the algae without fixing the nutrients and it simply comes back — often worse.

Fixing green water (pea-soup)

Green water is caused by free-floating single-celled algae. The fastest reliable fixes, in order of impact:

  1. Add a UV clarifier. Water passes a UV bulb that damages algae cells so they clump and get filtered out. This is the single most effective tool for green water. Size roughly 10 watts per 1,000 gallons (see filtration guide) and replace the bulb yearly.
  2. Add plant competition. Floating plants like water lettuce and lilies soak up the same nutrients algae needs and shade the water — cover 40–60% of the surface in summer (pond plants guide).
  3. Cut feeding. Most ponds are fed 2–3× too much. Every pellet you drop becomes algae food.
  4. Do water changes. 10–20% weekly removes nitrate and phosphate that no filter removes (see the nitrogen cycle).
  5. Check your stocking. If you are overstocked, no amount of technology will keep up.

Expect 1–2 weeks for a UV clarifier to visibly clear the water. During that time keep mechanical filter media clean — dead algae cells will clog it.

Fixing string algae

String algae is attached, so a UV clarifier does not help it. The practical approach:

  • Remove it manually. A brush or rake pulls out long strands quickly; in severe cases, drain some water and scrub walls. This is normal seasonal maintenance, not a failure.
  • Shade the shallow areas. String algae loves warm, sunny shelves. Plants and a 40–60% surface cover starve it of light.
  • Reduce phosphate. Phosphate (from fish food and tap water) is string algae’s main fuel. Cut feeding, use a phosphate-absorbing media if your tests show high phosphate, and keep plants growing.
  • Avoid chemical algaecides as a habit. They kill algae fast but dump dead organic matter into the water, which rots into ammonia — a classic cause of fish stress right after treatment.
CautionA little string algae is actually good — it absorbs nutrients and gives fry and invertebrates shelter. Only remove it when it becomes a problem; don’t chase a perfectly bare pond.

The prevention checklist

HabitHow it helps
Cover 40–60% of surface with plants in summerShades water, competes for nutrients
Feed only what fish eat in 5 minutesCuts the nutrient supply at the source
Run a correctly sized UV clarifierSuppresses green water before it blooms
Weekly 10–20% water changesRemoves nitrate and phosphate
Keep bio-filter healthy (never clean it all at once)Stable water chemistry, less stress
Don’t overstockLess waste, less algae fuel

Combine these and algae stops being a recurring battle. If green water persists despite all of the above, the most common hidden cause is overstocking — be honest with yourself before spending more money on equipment.
