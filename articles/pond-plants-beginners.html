Best Pond Plants for Beginners (and How to Care for Them)

Plants~10 min readUpdated Sep 12, 2026

Plants are not decoration — they are the cheapest filtration you will ever buy. They shade the water, compete with algae for nutrients, and give fish cover from predators and sun. Here are the easiest species that actually survive in a koi pond.

The four plant jobs in a pond

  • Shading: floating leaves block sunlight, starving green-water algae.
  • Nutrient competition: plants soak up nitrate and phosphate — the same food algae needs.
  • Oxygen: healthy plants release oxygen during the day.
  • Shelter: fish use plants to hide from birds, sun and stress.
Reality checkPlants help, but they cannot replace a filter. A heavily stocked koi pond will still need proper filtration; plants are a supplement, not a substitute. See Pond Filtration Explained.

1. Floating plants (easiest, instant effect)

PlantWhy it’s greatWatch out for
Water lettuceGrows fast, superb at nutrient removal and shadingCan smother the surface — thin it out weekly
Water hyacinthBeautiful purple flowers, rapid growth, great shadeInvasive in warm regions; never release into the wild
Frogbit / water soldierNeat rosettes, easy to manage, good coverSlower growth; keep a few clumps

Floaters are the most beginner-proof plants: no planting, no pots, just drop them in. Remove a third of them every few weeks so they do not cover the entire surface.

2. Water lilies (the showpiece)

Lilies provide broad shade and flowers all summer. They grow from a rhizome planted in a wide, shallow pot of heavy clay soil, with the crown just above the soil surface and the pot placed so leaves float on the water.

  • Hardy lilies are easiest — they come back every year and tolerate a wide range of ponds.
  • Place the pot at the right depth for the variety (most: 12–24 in of water above the pot).
  • Give them 5–6 hours of sun for the best flowering.
  • Feed with slow-release aquatic fertiliser tablets in spring and midsummer.

3. Marginals (shallow-water edge plants)

Marginals grow in the shallow shelf at the pond edge and soften the whole look:

  • Pickerel weed — blue spikes, easy, great with koi.
  • Dwarf cattail — vertical accent, but choose the dwarf variety (common cattail takes over).
  • Iris (Louisiana/Japanese) — tough, colourful and koi-resistant.
  • Marsh marigold — early spring flowers, very hardy.

4. Oxygenating plants (submerged)

Submerged plants like anacharis (elodea), hornwort and vallisneria release oxygen into the water and provide fry cover. Koi love to nibble them, so plant them in bunches weighted with rocks or in mesh pots, and expect to replant occasionally.

Living with koi: what survives and what doesn’t

Koi are bulldozers. They uproot, dig and eat almost anything soft. Some practical rules:

  • Protect the roots: plant in wide, heavy pots topped with gravel — koi cannot dig under a gravel cap.
  • Choose koi-proof favourites: water lettuce, hyacinth (floating, no roots to dig), lilies in pots, pickerel weed and iris all cope well.
  • Expect some grazing: a little nibbling is fine; it adds fibre to their diet.
  • Net during autumn leaf-fall: and remove spent plant material before winter — see Winter Koi Pond Care.
TipCover 40–60% of the water surface with plant shade in summer. This single habit dramatically reduces algae, keeps water cooler, and makes koi feel secure.
