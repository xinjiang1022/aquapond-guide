Spring Pond Startup: The Complete Opening Checklist

Seasonal Care~10 min readUpdated Sep 14, 2026

More koi die in spring than in winter. After months at low temperatures, fish are weak, the filter bacteria have partially died back, and warming water wakes up parasites and bacteria at the same time. A slow, careful opening routine turns the danger season into a smooth one.

Why spring is the danger season

Three things collide every spring:

  • Weak fish: after months of minimal metabolism, koi have little energy reserve and a suppressed immune system.
  • A broken cycle: filter bacteria slow and partially die in cold water, so the pond’s biological filtration is weaker than it was in autumn (see the nitrogen cycle).
  • Waking pathogens: parasites and bacteria become active as water warms — exactly when fish are least able to fight them.
The golden rule of springSlow and steady. Every step of opening the pond should be gentler than you think it needs to be — especially feeding and filter cleaning.

Step 1: Assess before you touch anything

  • Walk the pond: check the liner, coping stones, skimmer and returns for winter damage.
  • Check all fish are present and swimming normally — no gasping, flashing or clamped fins.
  • Remove winter debris: leaves, dead plant matter and sludge that accumulated under the ice. Rotting debris is an ammonia factory.
  • Do a small water test (ammonia, nitrite, pH) before restarting anything — it tells you what state the water is in.

Step 2: Restart equipment carefully

  • Pump: reconnect and test it in a bucket first if it was stored dry. If it was running all winter, just confirm flow.
  • Filter: clean one chamber or half the media — never the whole filter. Rinse media in pond water, not tap water, to preserve the surviving bacteria colony.
  • UV clarifier: clean the quartz sleeve and, if the bulb is 12+ months old, replace it — UV output fades even when the bulb still lights.
  • Aeration: run it 24/7. Fish and bacteria both need oxygen as the pond wakes up.

Step 3: Rebuild the nitrogen cycle

Treat a freshly opened pond like a new pond for the first 2–3 weeks:

  • Test ammonia and nitrite every day for the first week, then every 2–3 days.
  • If ammonia or nitrite rises above 0.5 ppm, do a 10–20% water change and hold off feeding.
  • A beneficial-bacteria product can help restart the colony — it seeds growth but does not replace the time needed.
  • Do not add new fish in spring until the cycle has stabilised (see water parameters).

Step 4: Feed by temperature, not by calendar

Water temperatureFeeding
Below 8–10 °CDo not feed — koi cannot digest properly in cold water
10–13 °COnce every 2–3 days, tiny amounts of wheatgerm food
13–18 °COnce a day, wheatgerm or low-protein food
Above 18 °CNormal summer feeding, introduced gradually

If a cold snap hits after you start feeding, stop again — temperature drops matter more than the calendar. Full details in the Koi Feeding Guide.

Step 5: Check fish health closely

Spring is when problems from winter surface. Watch daily for:

  • Flashing or rubbing (parasites — very common in spring)
  • White spots, cottony patches or red ulcers
  • Clamped fins, lethargy or isolating at the bottom
  • Rapid gill movement

If anything appears, test the water first — poor water mimics disease. If the water is fine and symptoms persist, a scrape and microscope check by a specialist is the fastest, cheapest way to an accurate treatment (see common koi diseases).

Step 6: Bring plants back gradually

  • Divide and repot lilies that have outgrown their pots; add slow-release aquatic fertiliser.
  • Reintroduce floaters once frost risk has passed.
  • Trim dead growth from marginals — but wait until you see new green shoots.

Follow this sequence and the spring opening becomes a calm two-week routine instead of a fish-loss event. For the winter that comes before it, see Winter Koi Pond Care.
