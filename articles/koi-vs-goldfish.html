Koi vs Goldfish: 8 Key Differences to Know Before You Buy

Care Guides~7 min readUpdated Sep 12, 2026

They both live in ponds, they both look golden — but koi and goldfish are very different commitments. This comparison tells you exactly what changes (budget, space, filtration, lifespan) when you choose one over the other.

The quick comparison

KoiGoldfish (common/shubunkin)
Adult size24–36 in (60–90 cm)10–14 in (25–35 cm) in a pond
Lifespan25–35+ years10–20 years
Minimum pond1,000–1,500 gal for a few~300–500 gal for a few
Waste outputVery highModerate
Typical price per fish$20–$100+ (show grade much more)$1–$10
Winter hardinessExcellent with depthExcellent with depth
Cold toleranceGreat — stops feeding below ~8 °CGreat — similar behaviour
Handling / bondingLearn to eat from your handAlso learn, but more skittish

The 8 differences that actually matter

1. Size is the whole game

A single adult koi can outweigh a dozen goldfish. Space, filtration and water changes all scale with fish mass — a koi pond is fundamentally a bigger, more powerful system. If you are building small (under ~1,000 gallons), goldfish are the honest choice.

2. Filtration demand

Koi produce up to 5–10× more waste per fish than goldfish. A koi pond needs a properly sized bio-filter and high turnover (see our filtration guide); a goldfish pond gets away with much lighter equipment.

3. Cost of entry

It is rarely the fish that costs money — it is the pond. Building for koi (volume, pump, filter, UV) typically costs 3–5× more than a comparable goldfish pond. Budget honestly before choosing.

4. Lifespan and commitment

Koi outlive most dogs and many marriages. Buying koi is a 25–35 year commitment with the infrastructure to match. Goldfish are a shorter, more forgiving commitment — sensible for a first pond.

5. Cold-weather behaviour

Both handle freezing winters if the pond is deep enough (3–4 ft). In winter both stop feeding; koi simply get slower at slightly higher temperatures. Read our winter care checklist either way.

6. Temperament and interaction

Koi are famously bold — they greet you, eat from your hand, and become genuinely interactive pets. Goldfish are prettier in a different way but stay more distant. If "pet" energy is what you want, koi win.

7. Variety and appeal

Koi have hundreds of named varieties (Kohaku, Showa, Sanke, Ogon…) and serious hobbyist culture. Goldfish have their own charm — fantails, orandas and shubunkins — at a fraction of the price.

8. Can they live together?

Yes — goldfish and koi can share a pond, and many keepers mix them. Two rules: the pond must be sized for koi (goldfish become snacks in a too-crowded koi pond), and always quarantine newcomers. See stocking density before mixing.

So which should you choose?

Choose goldfish if…you are new to ponds, your pond is under ~1,000 gallons, or your budget for pumps/filters is modest. Goldfish give you the full pond experience at 20% of the cost.
Choose koi if…you want an interactive, long-term pet, you can build 1,500+ gallons, and you are ready to invest in proper filtration. If you can only afford the fish but not the filtration — choose goldfish.
