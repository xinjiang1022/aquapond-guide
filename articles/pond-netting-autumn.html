Pond Netting: Keeping Autumn Leaves Out of Your Koi Pond

Pond Design~7 min readUpdated Nov 7, 2026

Leaving netting out in autumn feels like extra work until you lift it in spring and see what it saved you — a pond bottom that is not a compost heap. A few weeks of leaf fall can overload a small pond before winter even begins. Here is what netting actually buys you and how to fit it without blocking the whole pond.

Why autumn leaves are a real problem

Fallen leaves are not just messy on the surface. As they rot underwater they consume oxygen and release nutrients — exactly the kind of load a pond is least ready for when the filter slows down in cool weather. Over winter the same leaves break down into the kind of bottom sludge covered in the sludge guide, and spring starts with cloudy water you did not have to create. Netting is cheap insurance against all of it.

What mesh size to choose

MeshGood forTrade-off
Fine (under 1 cm)Small leaves, also stops herons and predatorsCatches debris on top; needs clearing more often
Medium (1–2 cm)Most deciduous leaves — the usual choiceSmall debris can slip through
Large (over 3 cm)Big leaves only, cheap and lightWon't hold oak, beech or maple small leaves
TipMedium black mesh is the standard recommendation: it catches the leaves that matter, stays out of sight, and still lets light through. See predator protection if you are also worried about herons — fine mesh doubles as a deterrent.

How to install it so it stays usable

Laying netting flat on the water looks tidy but sags as leaves collect. Stretch it over hoops or a simple frame raised 30–50 cm above the surface instead — leaves slide or blow off, water stays aerated, and you can still reach the pond. Leave one edge as a hinged flap with a couple of clips: that becomes your feeding hatch through winter, so you are not unclipping the whole net every time the koi want food. Weight the edges so a gust does not dump half a hedge onto the water.

When to take it off

Once the trees are bare and the major leaf fall is over, the net can come off — usually late autumn in temperate climates, not mid-winter. Clear it, rinse it, and store it dry. Keep an eye on winter care after that: with the leaves out of the equation, the rest of the winter routine is mostly about keeping an opening in the ice.
