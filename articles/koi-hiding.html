Why Your Koi Are Hiding (and What to Do About It)

Fish Health~8 min readUpdated Nov 8, 2026

Koi are usually the most confident fish in the pond. When they suddenly press against the wall, tuck under plants, or stop coming up for food, something has changed. Most of the time the cause is simple — and checking the right things in the right order saves you from panicking about disease.

Normal shyness vs a real problem

BehaviourHow to read it
Newly added koi hiding for a few daysNormal — they are settling in (see quarantine)
Hiding only at feeding time near peopleSkittishness, not illness — common in young or recently moved fish
Sudden hiding in confident fishA real change — investigate
Hiding plus gasping, clamped fins, or spotsTreat as a health issue — see diseases
TipStart with the simplest explanation before the scariest one. Koi hide for environmental reasons far more often than for disease.

The common causes, in order

Something new in the pond

A new pump, a new plant basket, a net left in the water, or a predator (heron, cat) visiting recently — koi react strongly to change. If the hiding started right after you did something, that is almost certainly the cause.

Water quality

Stressed water is the next suspect. Test ammonia, nitrite and pH (see testing routine and parameters). A recent water change, a filter that has not been cleaned in months, or warm weather can all push fish into hiding.

Temperature and season

In very hot or very cold water, koi naturally become less active and stay deeper. This is normal behaviour, not a problem — see winter care and cold-water feeding.

Predators

If a heron or cat has visited, koi will stay tight to the bottom and edges for days. Check for signs and consider netting or cover (see predator protection).

What to do first

Test the water, watch for a full day or two, and do not rush to medicate. Most sudden hiding resolves itself once the cause (a visitor, a water change, a cold snap) passes. If the hiding lasts more than a few days, or comes with visible symptoms, that is when to treat it as a health issue rather than a mood.

If they keep hiding after everything checks out

Sometimes the water is fine, no predator visited, and the fish still prefer the deep end. That is often a confidence issue rather than a health one — young koi, fish moved recently, or ponds without much cover can stay skittish for weeks. Give them places to hide (plants, bog areas — see plant zones), avoid sudden movements at the edge, and feed consistently from the same spot. Koi usually become bolder as they learn the pond is safe. If a previously bold fish stays withdrawn for more than a couple of weeks even with everything "normal", that is when it stops being a behaviour question and becomes a health one.
