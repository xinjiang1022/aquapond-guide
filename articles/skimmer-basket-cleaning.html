How Often to Clean a Pond Skimmer Basket

Maintenance~6 min readUpdated Nov 14, 2026

Of all the routine pond jobs, the skimmer basket is the easiest to forget — and the one that slows everything down the moment it clogs. It sits quietly at the surface, catches leaves and debris, and quietly starves your filter of flow the second it gets full. (See the broader guide to pond skimmers.)

What the skimmer basket does

The skimmer pulls surface water — where leaves, pollen and floating gunk collect — through a basket before it reaches the filter. A clean basket means the filter gets the flow it needs; a clogged one means less water moves, the filter works harder, and debris sinks to the bottom instead of being caught (see bottom sludge).

How often to clean it

ConditionSuggested check
Calm season, few leavesCheck the basket roughly weekly
Autumn leaf dropCheck more often — it fills fast
Noticeably weaker skimmer flowClean it now, don't wait for the schedule
TipThink in terms of "watch the flow" rather than a fixed date. When the surface skims less effectively, the basket wants emptying.

How to clean it

Lift the basket, empty the debris, and rinse it so water can pass through again. It is a quick job — which is exactly why letting it pile up is such an easy mistake. Pair it with checking your filter (see filter maintenance) on the same visit so the two stay in step.

Don't over-clean the biological part

Emptying the basket is fine — it is mechanical debris. Just avoid scrubbing your filter media in tap water, because that washes away the beneficial bacteria the pond relies on (see the nitrogen cycle). Rinse filter media in pond water instead. The basket can be emptied freely; the biological filter is the part to treat gently.
