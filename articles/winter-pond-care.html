Winter Koi Pond Care: A Complete Checklist

Seasonal Care~10 min readUpdated Sep 12, 2026

Healthy koi survive freezing winters — wild carp have done it for thousands of years. What kills them is not the cold itself but three winter mistakes: feeding in cold water, letting the pond freeze solid, and leaving debris to rot under the ice. Here is the complete winter routine.

The three rules of winter koi keeping

  1. No food below ~8–10 °C (46–50 °F). Koi cannot digest in cold water; food rots inside them.
  2. Depth beats everything. The pond must be at least 3 ft (90 cm) deep, ideally 4 ft (1.2 m) in cold climates, so fish can sit in stable water near the bottom.
  3. Keep a hole in the ice. A sealed ice sheet traps toxic gases from decomposing waste. You need a small open area for gas exchange.

Autumn preparation (weeks before the first frost)

Switch to wheatgerm food as temperatures fallEasy-to-digest food builds safe winter energy stores

Equipment decisions for winter

  • Pump: in mild climates you can run the pump all winter to keep a hole open. In hard-freeze climates, many keepers switch to an air pump instead — a waterfall freezing into a solid column can damage the pump and plumbing.
  • Air pump with diffuser: the safest all-winter tool. Bubbles keep a small area of surface moving, which prevents full icing and maintains gas exchange.
  • De-icer (pond heater): a floating de-icer keeps a small hole open. Do not use it to heat the pond — the goal is a breathing hole, not warm water.
  • Never break ice by force: the shockwave can kill fish. Use the de-icer or pour a little warm water to melt a hole.
CriticalDo not use a solid pond cover that seals the surface completely. Fish need gas exchange all winter. If you cover the pond, always leave a vent and keep aeration running underneath.

What fish do all winter

Below ~10 °C koi gather at the bottom and barely move. Their metabolism drops to near zero: heart rate slows, they stop eating and they hover in the deepest, most stable water. This is normal — do not panic, do not feed them, do not poke them.

If you see fish swimming actively under the ice or at the surface all winter, the pond is probably too shallow or the water is degrading. Test for ammonia a few times over winter — even under ice, it can climb.

Spring restart (the danger season)

More koi die in spring than in winter. After months of slow metabolism, fish are weak, and the warming water wakes up parasites and bacteria at the same time.

  1. Restart the pump and clean the filter gently — never all at once.
  2. Test ammonia and nitrite daily for the first two weeks; the bacteria colony may have partially died back.
  3. Do not feed until water is reliably above 10 °C — and start with tiny wheatgerm meals.
  4. Watch for flashing or lethargy; a spring scrape by a specialist is cheap insurance after a hard winter.
