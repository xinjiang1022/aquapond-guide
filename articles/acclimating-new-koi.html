How to Acclimate New Koi to Pond Water

Fish Health~7 min readUpdated Nov 12, 2026

The bag arrives, you open it, and the temptation is to tip the koi straight into the pond. Resist that. New fish have just survived a stressful ride, and a sudden jump in temperature or water chemistry is exactly the kind of shock that turns an exciting day into a loss. Acclimation takes half an hour and prevents most of it. (After this, see quarantine.)

Why acclimation matters

ShockWhat happens
Temperature shockBag water and pond water differ — a fast change stresses the fish
Water chemistry shockThe shop's water and your pond water are different waters
Handling stressNetting and transport already wore the fish out
TipAcclimation is about giving the fish time to match temperature and chemistry gradually, not about helping them "get used to" the pond.

How to acclimate

Float the sealed bag on the pond surface to equalise temperature. After a while, open the bag and gradually mix small amounts of pond water into it over several minutes, letting the fish adjust. This slow mixing is the step that prevents the chemistry shock. Choose healthy fish to begin with (see choosing koi), and never tip the transport water into your pond.

After release

Let the fish swim out on their own rather than tipping them. They will likely hide or stay low for the first day or two — that is normal (see why koi hide). Do not feed heavily at first, and watch closely. A calm release and a quiet first week make a huge difference to whether new koi settle in well.

How long should acclimation take

There is no exact minute count, but the idea is to go slowly rather than fast. Float the bag until the water temperatures feel roughly equal, then add pond water in small, gradual steps over a period of time rather than dumping a big splash at once. Rushing this is the whole point of the exercise — if the water temperatures are very different on the day you bring fish home, give it more patience, not less. A longer, calmer acclimation on arrival day pays off over the fish's whole settling-in week.
