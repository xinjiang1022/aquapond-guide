Live Aquarium Plants for Beginners: 12 Easy Species

Plants~11 min readUpdated Sep 12, 2026

Live plants are the best upgrade a beginner tank can get: they absorb the same nutrients algae feeds on, give fish cover, and make the tank look alive. The trick is choosing species that grow in plain conditions — no CO2, no special lights. These twelve all do.

Why live plants (and why beginners fail)

Most "plant failures" are not the keeper's fault — they are the plant's. Fast-growing, demanding species like carpeting plants need CO2 injection and strong lighting. Beginners should choose slow, hardy, low-light species instead. They grow slowly, forgive mistakes, and still do the job.

Plants also matter for water quality: they compete with algae for nitrate and phosphate, which is the same reason pond plants help outdoor ponds.

The "rhizome rule" (where beginners go wrong)

Java fern and anubias grow from a thick horizontal stem called a rhizome. If you bury it in the gravel, it rots and dies. Instead, glue or tie the rhizome to a piece of wood or rock. This one rule is responsible for half of all "my plants keep dying" stories.

Getting started: a simple setup

  1. Substrate: standard aquarium gravel is fine for the plants above (rooted types benefit from a root tab pushed under them every few months).
  2. Light: 6–8 hours per day on a timer. More light without CO2 means more algae, not more plants.
  3. Fertiliser: a basic liquid fertiliser once or twice a week helps stems and floaters; start with half doses.
  4. Stock lightly first: plants grow into their space. Start with 60–70% of the look you want.
  5. Expect melt: crypts and some swords often melt when moved to new water. Remove the dead leaves and wait — new growth follows.
TipFast-growing plants (hornwort, anacharis, wisteria) are your algae weapons. In a new tank, a bunch of hornwort floating near the light will out-compete algae for nutrients while your slower plants establish.

Plants and your fish

  • Goldfish and cichlids eat and dig — choose tough, fast plants (anubias on rock, java fern, hornwort).
  • Livebearers and tetras love plants — plenty of cover reduces stress and aggression.
  • Shrimp and snails thrive on plants and keep them clean; a planted tank is their natural home.
  • Quarantine new plants — rinse them and, ideally, soak them for a few days in a separate container to avoid bringing in pest snails or algae.
