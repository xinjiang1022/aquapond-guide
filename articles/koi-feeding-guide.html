Koi Feeding Guide: How Much and How Often to Feed

Care Guides~8 min readUpdated Sep 12, 2026

Koi are always hungry — that is exactly why overfeeding is the number one cause of pond deaths. The right amount depends on water temperature, not on how eagerly the fish beg. Here is the simple system professionals use.

The 5-minute rule

The basic rule for warm-season feeding: give only as much food as the koi can eat in 5 minutes, and remove anything left over. In practice, that is usually 2–4% of the fish's body weight per day, split into 2–3 meals.

TipWatch the fish, not the food. If food is still sinking after a few minutes, you have already overfed. A feeding ring or floating pellet trap makes leftovers easy to scoop out.

Feed by temperature, not by calendar

Koi are cold-blooded: their metabolism follows the water. This table is the feeding schedule most koi keepers follow through the year:

Water temperatureFeedingFood type
Below 8 °C (46 °F)Do not feed—
8–12 °C (46–54 °F)Once every 2–3 days, tiny amountsWheatgerm / low-protein "cold water" food
12–18 °C (54–64 °F)Once a dayWheatgerm, or mixed
18–25 °C (64–77 °F)2–3 times a dayGrowth / high-protein summer food
Above 25 °C (77 °F)1–2 times a day, smaller mealsHigh-protein, but watch oxygen in heatwaves

Two golden rules behind the table:

  • Cold water slows digestion. Below ~10 °C, food can rot inside the fish — feeding in winter literally kills koi.
  • Temperature drops are more important than the reading itself. When a cold snap arrives, cut feeding immediately, even if the average is still warm.

Choosing the right food

  • Protein level: 30–38% protein in summer growth food; 20–25% wheatgerm food for spring/autumn. High protein in cold water = waste and kidney strain.
  • Pellet size: match pellet size to the largest mouth in the pond. A mixed-size pellet (or soaking pellets briefly) helps smaller fish compete.
  • Soak before feeding: dry pellets swell in the stomach; soaking for 5–10 minutes prevents digestive problems, especially in cooler water.
  • Treats (optional): blanched peas, lettuce, orange slices and brine shrimp are fine occasionally — but treats count toward the 5-minute budget.

What overfeeding actually does

Uneaten food does not just waste money. It:

  • Rotts into ammonia — the toxic waste your filter has to process (see the nitrogen cycle).
  • Fuels algae blooms and cloudy water.
  • Feeds parasites and bacteria, because stressed fish in dirty water get sick.
  • Can cause constipation and swim bladder issues in the fish themselves.
WarningIf your ammonia test never reads zero, or your water is permanently green or cloudy, the first thing to cut is almost always food — not the filter, not the pump.

Special situations

  • New fish: feed very little for the first 3–5 days while they adjust; a hungry koi is a calm koi.
  • Sick fish: stop feeding during treatment — fish can go weeks without food, and less waste means less stress on the pond.
  • Autumn: gradually switch to wheatgerm food as temperatures fall; fattening koi for winter with high-protein food is a common mistake.
  • Heatwaves: warm water holds less oxygen. Feed smaller meals, earlier in the day, and keep aeration running.
