Why Your Koi Won’t Eat (and What to Do)

Fish Health~7 min readUpdated Nov 10, 2026

Koi are famously greedy, so a sudden refusal of food is one of the clearest signals in pond keeping. Loss of appetite is a symptom, not a diagnosis on its own — but once you check the basics, the cause is usually obvious.

Normal off-food vs a real problem

SituationWhat it means
Cold water (autumn/winter)Normal — metabolism slows; reduce or stop feeding (see cold-water feeding)
Newly added koiNormal settling-in behaviour (see quarantine)
Sudden off-food in confident fishCheck water quality and stress factors
Off-food plus clamped fins, spots, gaspingTreat as illness — see diseases
TipAlways check the season and temperature first. Many "off food" worries in autumn are just the pond winding down for winter.

The common causes

Water quality is the next suspect — test ammonia, nitrite and pH (see testing routine). Stress comes next: a predator visit, a recent water change, or a net left in the pond. Koi that are hiding as well as refusing food are showing stress rather than disease (see why koi hide).

What to do

Do not rush to medicate or overfeed to tempt them. Test the water, watch for a day or two, and feed sparingly from the same spot. Most temporary off-food episodes resolve once the stressor passes. If the refusal lasts more than a few days, or comes with visible symptoms, that is when it becomes a health issue rather than a mood.

When to actually worry

A temporary off-food day after a cold snap or a new fish is not a crisis. The line to take seriously is when the refusal lasts more than a few days and the fish look unwell — clamped fins, unusual spots, gasping at the surface, or listlessness. That combination points to illness rather than a mood, and testing the water plus watching closely is what tells the two apart. When in doubt, it is better to feed lightly and watch than to flood the pond with food the fish will not eat.
