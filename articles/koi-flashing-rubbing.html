Koi Rubbing Rocks: What Flashing Really Means

Fish Health~6 min readUpdated Nov 18, 2026

You are watching the pond and a koi suddenly shoots sideways, scraping itself along a rock or the bottom and then darting off. Keepers call this "flashing," and it is usually the first sign that something is irritating the fish. It is not always an emergency — but it is worth paying attention to. (See also common koi diseases.)

What flashing looks like

It is a quick, deliberate sideways flick rather than normal swimming. The fish seems to be trying to scratch its flank or its gill cover against something hard. A one-off flick is not alarming; repeated flashing, especially by several fish, is the signal to investigate.

Common causes

CauseNotes
Parasites or skin irritationThe classic reason — microscopic irritants on the skin
Poor water qualityHigh ammonia or nitrite irritates gills and skin (see water parameters)
Rapid change after a water changeSudden shifts can stress fish (see after a water change)
Normal curiosityOccasional flicking can be harmless
ImportantThis is general information, not a diagnosis. Do not start adding treatments to the pond on a guess — wrong treatments can make things worse. Test the water first, and involve a vet or specialist for persistent flashing.

What to do next

Start simple: check your water quality, make sure the filtration and aeration are working, and watch whether the flashing continues or spreads. Healthy, stable water resolves a surprising amount. If flashing keeps happening, the fish stop eating (see koi not eating), or you see visible damage, it is time to get expert help rather than experimenting.

Normal vs serious

An occasional single flick, with the fish otherwise feeding normally and behaving well, is usually nothing to worry about — fish scratch themselves the way any animal does. It becomes serious when flashing is repeated several times an hour, spreads across multiple fish, or comes with other signs: clamped fins, loss of appetite, excess slime, gasping at the surface, or sores. When those cluster together, act rather than wait.
