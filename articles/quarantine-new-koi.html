How to Quarantine New Koi: A Step-by-Step Setup Guide

Fish Health~9 min readUpdated Oct 5, 2026

The most expensive mistake in koi keeping is skipping quarantine. One new fish carrying parasites can stress an entire established pond in days. A simple quarantine setup — no second pond required — protects everything you’ve built.

Why quarantine is non-negotiable

Fish from dealers, auctions and garden centres travel through shared systems. New fish can carry:

  • External parasites (ich, flukes, costia) that only show symptoms under stress — which travel creates.
  • Bacterial and viral issues that may be dormant until the fish settles into your pond.
  • General stress — a fish that arrives weak will fare far better alone than competing in a new pond.

Even "clean-looking" fish are a risk. Quarantine is cheap insurance: a few weeks of patience versus treating your whole pond later (koi diseases guide).

Golden ruleNever add a new fish directly to your established pond. Every new arrival quarantines — no exceptions, no matter how healthy it looks.

How long should quarantine last?

The practical minimum is 2–3 weeks, and 4 weeks is better for peace of mind. Many parasites and problems surface within the first 2 weeks of stress; a 3–4 week window catches most of them while giving the fish time to recover from travel.

  • 2 weeks: absolute minimum for observing obvious problems.
  • 3–4 weeks: recommended — covers the typical parasite life-cycle window and lets the fish re-acclimatise to feeding.
  • Watch, don’t just wait: quarantine is observation time. Check behaviour, appetite, fins, gills and droppings daily.

Setting up a quarantine tank without a second pond

You don’t need an expensive hospital pond. A large stock tank, tub or even a sturdy plastic container works — size it so one or two koi can turn around comfortably. Minimum essentials:

ItemWhy it matters
Dedicated filtrationA small filter you run only for quarantine — never shared with the main pond to avoid cross-contamination
AerationAir pump + air stone running 24/7; quarantine tanks are small and can drop oxygen fast (aeration guide)
Water conditionerDechlorinator and a basic water test kit for ammonia, nitrite and pH (parameters guide)
Cover / netKoi are skilled jumpers — cover the tank, especially overnight
Dedicated toolsYour own net and bucket for the quarantine tank, kept separate from pond equipment

The step-by-step flow

  1. Set up first: fill the tank, run filtration and aeration, condition the water, and let it settle for a day before the fish arrives.
  2. Acclimatise slowly: float the bag for 15–20 minutes to match temperature, then mix a little tank water into the bag before releasing. Do not dump bag water into the tank.
  3. Observe quietly: keep light low for the first 24–48 hours. Don’t feed on day one; offer tiny amounts from day two if the fish is interested.
  4. Test daily: small tanks spike ammonia fast. If ammonia or nitrite rises, do small water changes and hold feeding.
  5. Watch for red flags: flashing, gasping, clamped fins, white spots, or lethargy — act early or consult a specialist.
  6. Release when ready: after 3–4 clean, stable weeks, net the fish into the pond gently and keep a close eye on it for a few more days.
Pro tipRun the quarantine tank's filter media in the main pond's filter for a couple of weeks before quarantine starts — it will be colonised with beneficial bacteria, so the quarantine tank cycles far faster.

Common mistakes

  • Quarantining in the pond itself — a netted-off pond corner still shares water, parasites and disease. It is not quarantine.
  • Sharing nets and buckets — the fastest way to defeat the whole exercise.
  • Releasing "early because it looks fine" — many parasites are invisible for 2+ weeks.
  • Treating during quarantine without a diagnosis — random medication adds stress and breeds resistance; observe first, treat with a plan.
  • Skipping water tests — small quarantine tanks fail faster than ponds; ammonia is the silent killer.

The payoff

Quarantine is the difference between adding a fish and gambling the whole pond. Three quiet weeks of patience, clean water and observation protect your existing koi, your water quality and your budget — and the new fish gets to arrive healthy, settled and hungry.
