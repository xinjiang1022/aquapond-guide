How to Patch a Small Pond Liner Leak

Pond Setup~7 min readUpdated Nov 12, 2026

Losing water faster than evaporation? Before you panic and replace the whole liner, check whether it is actually a leak. Many "leaks" are splashing, waterfall spray or an overfull skimmer — and the small number that are real liner damage are usually a simple patch, not a rebuild. (See also the general guide to choosing a liner.)

Find the leak first

SymptomWhat to check
Water level drops steadilyMark the level, leave the pump off, and see where it stops — that reveals the leak height
Wet patch outside the linerTrace back to the liner edge or seam
Loss only when pump runsOften a waterfall/pipe seal, not the liner itself
TipAlways rule out splash and evaporation before you drain the pond. A steady level drop with the pump off is the real clue.

Patch materials

Use a repair kit made for the liner material you have (EPDM rubber, PVC or HDPE — they do not mix). The patch needs to overlap the tear by a good margin on every side, and the surface must be clean and dry — grease, algae and pond muck are what make patches fail later (see bottom sludge).

How to patch

Drain just enough water to expose the tear, clean and dry the area, apply the patch per the kit instructions, and press it firmly flat. Refill slowly and watch the patched spot for a day. Done properly, a small patch lasts for years — far cheaper and gentler on fish than draining and relining (see water changes).
