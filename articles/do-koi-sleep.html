Do Koi Sleep?

Fish Health~6 min readUpdated Nov 13, 2026

On a quiet evening, your koi may suddenly seem to drift low in the water, barely moving. They are not dead, sick, or stressed — they are resting. Koi do "sleep," but not like we do: no closing eyes, no deep unconsciousness, and they stay alert enough to bolt if disturbed.

How koi rest

Normal restingWhat it looks like
TimingMostly quieter, slower periods — often at night or in low light
PositionLower in the water column, almost hovering
ReactionStill alert — a light disturbance sends them moving
TipA fish that rests low and slow but darts away when you approach is resting normally. One that stays put even when approached needs a closer look (see hiding behaviour).

Normal rest vs a problem

Normal resting is calm, brief, and reversible. The warning signs are the ones that do not fit the pattern: a fish lying on its side, staying near the surface gasping, refusing food for days, or looking physically off (see not eating). Resting behaviour is part of a healthy koi's day — a body that never rests, or a body that cannot be roused, are both worth checking.

Why a calm night matters

Because koi rest in low light, a pond that is left undisturbed in the evening helps them settle well. Over-feeding late in the day, loud activity right up to dark, or constantly shining light into the pond keeps them "on" when they should be winding down. Feeding earlier in the day (see feeding guide) and letting the pond go quiet at night supports normal rest.
