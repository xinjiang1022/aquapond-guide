Do Koi Eat Snails, Frogs and Other Pond Life?

Koi Care~6 min readUpdated Nov 22, 2026

Koi are natural foragers. In the pond they root through the bottom, browse plant leaves and mouth anything small enough to fit, which means they do interact with the snails, frogs, insects and small fish that share the water. The short answer: yes, koi will eat small, slow or soft-bodied pond life they can catch, but plenty of animals coexist safely once you understand what size and what sort of creature you are dealing with. This guide separates the likely meals from the likely survivors.

Why koi forage the way they do

Koi are omnivores with a wide, opportunistic diet. In nature they would graze algae, nibble plant matter, pick at insect larvae, hunt small crustaceans and scavenge whatever is available. In a garden pond this instinct shows up as rooting along the floor, pecking at leaves and testing small objects. Their mouth is built to suck and probe rather than to tear apart large prey, so what they can eat comes down mostly to size, softness and whether the creature can get away.

Feeding the koi a balanced pond diet keeps this foraging urge partially satisfied; a well-fed koi spends less effort hunting around the pond than a hungry one. The feeding guide covers portion size and what to offer through the seasons.

What koi are likely to eat

The following are animals that commonly fall to koi in an outdoor pond, especially when they are young, small or slow-moving:

Pond creatureTypical outcome with koiNotes
Small / young pond snailsOften eatenTiny snails with soft or thin shells are easy to mouth; larger adults may survive
Tadpoles and newly metamorphosed frogletsOften picked offSmall and slow at this stage; mature frogs are usually too quick and large to catch
Insect larvae and pupaeCommonly eatenWrigglers, bloodworms and similar larvae are natural koi prey
Small crustaceans, water fleas and shrimpOften eatenSmall invertebrates are a natural part of a forager's diet
Very small or slow fish, fryMay be eatenNewborn fry and slow-moving small fish are vulnerable in a koi pond

What usually survives alongside koi

Not every pond animal is on the menu. Several groups coexist with koi once they are past the vulnerable early stages:

  • Large, mature snails with thick shells: A hard, full-grown shell is hard for a koi to crush, though the fish may still mouth and nudge it.
  • Adult frogs and toads: Fast, mobile and outside the size range koi can handle; many ponds welcome them for mosquito and insect control.
  • Larger, established fish: Fish that are too big to be treated as prey generally coexist, though mixing species still depends on temperament and water needs. Compare this in the koi and goldfish guide.
  • Well-established aquatic plants: Mature plants often hold their own; young shoots and tender leaves are more likely to be browsed. See plants koi tend to leave alone.
TipIf you deliberately add snails or other invertebrates to help tidy algae, expect some to become koi food over time. They are a partial cleanup crew, not a permanent guaranteed population — a point our algae-eating animals guide covers in more detail.

How foraging affects the pond balance

Koi's digging and browsing do shape the pond community. By eating larvae and small invertebrates they can trim the insect population; by rooting the floor they can stir up settled mulm, which is why adequate filtration and regular pond maintenance matter. A balanced pond does not need a large population of helper animals to stay clean — the filtration, stocking level and feeding routine carry most of that load.

If you are hoping to rear tadpoles, keep fry or maintain a thriving snail colony, the pond's open structure works against those goals. Dense plant cover, shallow refuge zones or a separate rearing container give small animals somewhere to escape the foraging fish.

Common questions about koi and pond wildlife

Will koi kill all the snails in my pond?

Usually not entirely. Small snails may be picked off, but mature ones often persist, and snails reproduce quickly enough that a steady population can remain in a well-planted pond. Heavy koi stocking and little plant cover shift the odds more against them.

Do koi eat frogspawn or tadpoles?

Newly laid spawn and small tadpoles are vulnerable; larger tadpoles and adult frogs are less so. If local frogs are a valued part of your garden, dense marginal plants and shallow escape routes help them survive.

Can I add helper shrimp or snails to an established koi pond?

You can, but treat them as a temporary, small-scale addition rather than a guaranteed colony. Check water parameters and never release leftover pond animals into local natural waterways, where they can become invasive.

WarningWildlife that appears in your pond should be handled gently. Avoid introducing non-native species, and never dispose of unwanted fish or invertebrates in streams, lakes or drains. For sick or injured pond animals, seek advice from an aquatic veterinarian or local pond specialist.
